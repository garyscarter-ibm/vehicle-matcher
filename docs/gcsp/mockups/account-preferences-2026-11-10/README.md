# Account and Preferences discussion mockup

Requested by Russell, 10 November 2026. This is an isolated interactive design proposal for discussion before User Stories. No production application, data model, recommendations, active policy, existing Story contract or dispatcher changes are authorised by this artifact. Native Jira Epic [FUCQ-358](https://ibm-consulting.atlassian.net/browse/FUCQ-358) was created and read back as **Improve Account and simplify Preferences**, To Do, label `backlog`, default Medium priority. This document is discussion context, not an alternative backlog. User Stories remain unwritten.

## Epic text submitted to Jira

**Improve Account and simplify Preferences**

Help an employee express what matters in their next car directly on their Account page, with a wider, more beautiful current-car presentation and a compact, intuitive Preferences component.

Use available desktop space, enlarge the current-car image without substantially changing the current-car facts, and remove the representative-image label. Let the employee rate their current car from one to five stars and use that feedback in recommendations: favour a similar or better car when they love it, and avoid similar recommendations when they dislike it.

Replace the large Preferences navigation card with the Preferences component itself. Remove “Cars which suit your preferences” from this stage; recommendations belong in Offer. Show categories first, reveal a category's options when selected, and make considered categories visibly distinct. Allow removal of a category using a bin control with confirmation. Require selections in at least three categories.

Provide “Same again, please!” to override preferences and disable the category controls. Prominently display a Buddy understanding score from 1–11, increasing with preference input to a maximum of 10; Same again sets it to 11. Use genuine model confidence if a suitable score exists. Keep the component around the footprint of Your cars. Reuse or extend shared components and enforce the UI ratchet. Use Impeccable for the design. Review the embedded Account-page mockup before writing User Stories; do not build the feature yet.

## Mockup refinements from discussion

Russell's subsequent direction, 10 November 2026, was added to FUCQ-358 as comment `3628581` and verified by native Jira readback:

- Keep the stars small and transparently over the vehicle image at every width. The revised control has 24px stars, 44px targets and accessible rating descriptions, without a separate visible rating card or caption. Subsequent screenshot feedback exposed letterboxing at intermediate widths; the image now keeps its intrinsic ratio and the overlay stays inside the actual bitmap, rather than only inside the media container.
- Use three-stop sliders for the four three-choice preferences, with “A bit of both” at the middle default. Body and Fuel keep their multiple-choice controls. The sliders reuse the existing native `renderRangeSlider` with a 0–100 range and a step of 50. Opening an unset slider category selects and automatically saves its midpoint; Done only closes the panel. Unopened categories are not counted.
- Save preferences automatically as they change. A bin action still requires confirmation before the deletion is saved.
- Replace Save Preferences with Engage Buddy / Disengage Buddy. Show a static green/red lamp and a text label at the top of Account. Editing preferences does not silently engage Buddy. The mockup retains its local engagement state on reload; it does not start a real search.

Further mockup feedback after that Jira comment: keep Buddy status and its lamp, remove both header allowance lines, and add **Points cost** to the current-car facts as **1,100 / 1,300 points per month** (current cost / budget). This row uses the full facts width. The earlier standalone current-cost line is removed. These refinements are captured here for the ongoing mockup discussion; no further Jira mutation or Story authoring has been performed.

Russell then replaced the importance disclosure and three-choice weight control with a single visible **This is really important** switch in the open category. A chosen preference has medium importance with the switch off and high importance with it on. An unchosen category is unimportant: it has no saved weight, and its switch is disabled and unchecked with “Choose a preference first.” Clearing the final Body/Fuel choice or confirming bin removal clears its importance; choosing again starts at medium. Cancelling removal, reloading or temporarily using Same again preserves existing high importance. Importance does not add categories or increase the illustrative understanding score. These agreed mockup semantics are captured locally for the ongoing discussion.

Further refinements use the existing amber warning tokens for high-importance categories, selected options, slider accents and the importance switch. Medium importance retains the normal blue selection. Same again removes both colours from saved categories while it overrides them; switching it off restores their prior emphasis. Its inline guidance is replaced by an information button beside the label, with the exact tooltip text “Select this option if you just love everything about your current car, and your next best car would be another {model and specification}”. Stars now sit at the right edge of the actual car image and show “How highly do you rate your {model and specification}?”. Both tooltips interpolate the same model/specification shown in the car heading, support hover, focus and touch, and dismiss with Escape or an outside tap. Long vehicle text wraps within the narrow viewport. These remain mockup discussion refinements, with no further Jira mutation.

Proposed interaction interpretation for discussion: the three-category minimum gates engaging Buddy, while incomplete selections still save. Same again bypasses that minimum. If an engaged Buddy loses its minimum because a category is removed or Same again is turned off, the mockup disengages it and explains the reason. This behaviour is a design proposal, not a change to a production contract.

## Findings and open decisions

- Current layout: `blocks/gcsp/employee/gcsp-employee.css` caps `.gcsp-rep` at 1120px with `margin: 0`. The shell owns the page inset. The proposal overrides the page composition inside the mockup only: a centred canvas up to 1680px and a flexible image column; facts remain around 480px wide.
- Existing `currentCarFeedback` stores only `preserve`, `change`, `state`, `source`. The strict validators in `server/gcsp/brief/index.js`, `server/gcsp/brief/editor.js`, `blocks/gcsp/brief/brief-controller.js` and recommendation input reject an added star rating today. A 1–5 current-car rating therefore needs an explicit contract/model extension. Preserve/change is not a star rating or observed after-use satisfaction.
- No suitable buddy/model confidence score was found in the current recommendation/brief contracts or code. Vehicle-evidence `confidence` describes source assurance; candidate fit is a different concept. The mockup deliberately uses an illustrative input-completeness mapping: no category = 1, one = 3, two = 4, three = 6, four = 7, five = 9, six = 10; Same again = 11. This is not a calibrated confidence estimate. Decide the production basis before authoring its Story.
- Six supported categories are used: body, fuel, sportiness, comfort, economy, size. Prototype qualitative choices for numeric axes illustrate simplification; they are not new active defaults. Preference position and importance remain independent. Russell's latest decision defines unchosen as unimportant, chosen as medium, and the separate “This is really important” switch as high. Production contract and adapter changes remain future Story scope.
- “At least three categories” is interpreted as three considered categories: selecting a Body/Fuel option or opening a three-stop category to accept its default midpoint. Same again bypasses that minimum. Untoggling restores the retained choices. Switching modes preserves the rating. Eligibility, affordability, essential needs and permission to search/hold must survive either mode in a future implementation.
- Rating is a signal, not permission to search, reserve or order. Define “similar or better”, the neutral three-star case and how a later essential need takes precedence before implementing matching behaviour.
- Preference, rating and engagement interactions save only to this mockup's own localStorage key. They never call production APIs. Reload keeps the local design state. Feedback is discussed in this Codex chat; there is no comment form or inaccessible feedback store.

## Component plan

Reuse the actual `portalShell`, navigation and `svgIcon`, Density tokens/font, `el`, `screenIntro`, `button`, `factList`, `mediaWell`, `renderOptionList`, `renderRangeSlider` and `checkboxField`. The native existing details pattern keeps secondary account information compact. No recommendation cards are mounted.

Proposed extensions are confined to this mockup: a switch treatment around `checkboxField`, category disclosure/selected/remove composition, a score readout, and a new accessible star-radio control because the kit has none. No reusable tooltip primitive exists in the current kit; one small prototype composition around native controls and the shared button/icon serves both help surfaces. None has been installed in the production kit. After agreement, the relevant Story must extend/add these in the shared kit with catalogue/gallery coverage, tests and a falling ratchet baseline; the mockup is not an exemption for production duplication.

## Open the mockup

Serve the repository with its existing static server, then open `docs/gcsp/mockups/account-preferences-2026-11-10/index.html`. Query `?state=selected` opens a three-category example; `?state=engaged` shows the green Buddy status; `?state=same` opens Same again; `?state=long` and `?state=missing` exercise content extremes. The default opens the local saved state or initial categories-only state; `?state=empty` resets this browser's local mockup state.
