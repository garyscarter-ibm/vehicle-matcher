# P02: Scheme ordering lead

**Status:** Provisional v0.1, 31 October 2026. Discovery input for FUCQ-2.

The business operator who plans the employee-scheme vehicle mix, submits requests to Production and manages the stock offered to employees. This role is distinct from an employee placing their own individual order. Its main evidence comes from the scheme-team discovery notes and process map. [E6](sources.md#e6-scheme-operations-research)

**Job statement — authored summary:** When I plan the next intake of scheme cars, help me balance what employees need with what Production can supply and the business can support, so renewals run smoothly without unwanted stock or excessive manual work.

## Responsibilities supported by evidence

- Review upcoming contract ends and previous vehicle selections, using a pivot table and the Spec Matrix to determine the mix to request.
- Submit the desired vehicle list to Production, receive the availability response and make the resulting selection available through the portal.
- Review unallocated stock and, where permitted, arrange movement to another channel.
- Coordinate with HR, Finance, Production, Alphabet and internal sales stakeholders whose objectives can conflict.

These responsibilities are documented in the 2024 discovery notes and Mural export. The notes describe a three-person team and an explicit aim to minimise manual orders. Historical scale estimates include approximately 7,500 scheme vehicles and 40–50 unallocated cars at a stock review; they are not current operating baselines. [E6](sources.md#e6-scheme-operations-research)

## Motivations

**Directly recorded:** reduce manual orders and workload; improve demand forecasting and employee satisfaction; reduce complaints; balance the scheme's role as a benefit and a business channel. Residual values and vehicle throughput also matter to the scheme. [E6](sources.md#e6-scheme-operations-research)

**Working hypotheses to validate:** wants confidence in the requested mix before committing; wants to distinguish genuine demand from choices made under stock pressure; wants understandable reasons for exceptions and a defensible basis for decisions. A small team is likely to value less rework as well as less data entry.

## Frictions and constraints

- The team must request a mix before employees make their final selections. Production may confirm only part of what was requested.
- Historic orders do not reliably reveal unmet demand or satisfaction. [E9](sources.md#e9-earlier-proposals)
- Employee interest can conflict with residual-value risk; the notes specifically mention EV demand and concerns about EV residual values.
- Unwanted stock consumes attention, while reported channel changes could restrict routes for clearing it. Their current applicability needs checking.
- Exceptional requests and customer-service enquiries compete for a small team's time. [E6](sources.md#e6-scheme-operations-research)

This role influences the business vehicle mix. The sources do not establish unilateral authority to change eligibility, points prices, Production capacity or allocation policy.

## What success could mean

Candidate measures grounded in the recorded objectives; definitions and targets are unverified:

| Outcome | Possible measure |
|---|---|
| Less manual administration | Manual orders and exceptions per renewal cycle; handling time. |
| A better match to employee demand | Satisfaction, unmet preferences and complaint volume. |
| Less unwanted stock | Unallocated volume and age; vehicles needing another disposal route. |
| Better planning | Difference between requested, confirmed and subsequently selected mix. |
| Commercially sustainable throughput | Timely renewals and channel volume, assessed alongside residual-value performance. |

Forecasting accuracy alone is insufficient: a forecast can accurately reproduce constrained past choices while missing what employees wanted.

## Discussion scenario

For discussion only: a renewal cohort needs cars, Production cannot supply the entire requested mix, and some older stock remains unselected. Explore how the lead decides which compromises are tolerable, who approves changes, and what information is missing. No allocation rule or ordering feature is agreed by this scenario.
