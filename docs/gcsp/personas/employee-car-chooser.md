## Meet Paula

Paula is capable, organised and quietly particular about the things she uses every day. She enjoys a good car: a comfortable seat, an unhurried motorway journey, a boot that takes the bags without a puzzle. Her Portimao Blue 320i M Sport Touring earns its place through usefulness as much as badge or appearance. She still looks back at it after parking. The heated wheel is a small pleasure on a cold start; the memory seat and a good playlist make Farnborough days feel less like a chore. Tight bays around Winchcombe are less welcome; she has a favourite space on the edge of the car park and little patience for discovering it has gone. These reactions to the recorded equipment are authored personality details.

She lives in Winchcombe and works for BMW Financial Services. Summit ONE in Farnborough houses BMW Financial Services alongside BMW UK and Alphabet. BMW advertises hybrid working in some Farnborough roles; that does not establish Paula's arrangement. For this scenario, picture two planned office days a week, home working and occasional workshops. Travelling from the Cotswolds to Hampshire makes an office visit something to organise around. [E12](sources.md#e12-farnborough-and-flexible-working)

Away from work, she likes getting outside and making a weekend of it. Walking kit, overnight bags and the occasional awkward purchase explain why she values the Touring's space. She would enjoy a change of car, but novelty alone is not a reason to give up something that already works.

## The next car has to fit the rest of her life

On a home-working day, the car may barely move. On an office day, comfort, a dependable journey and getting home without another task matter much more. A simple average mileage could miss that distinction. Her driveway has no chargepoint yet. Before choosing an electric alternative, she needs to check installation feasibility and a charging plan that works for those longer days. Workplace charging is not something she can assume.

Renewal brings a small project she has little appetite for. She has to compare what is available, understand the financial commitment and check whether a different shape of car really carries the same luggage. She also needs to check any other driver's requirements rather than assume that because she can drive it, everyone who needs it can. She enjoys the photographs and imagining the next cabin; she resents having to do the eligibility checks herself. A long preference questionnaire would feel like more work, even in an attractive new interface.

Her calendar is the immediate frustration. A stock release can coincide with a meeting or a planned journey. Repeatedly checking a portal feels like work, and she resents having to be unusually available to secure something ordinary and suitable. A rushed choice may leave her solving the consequences for months. Scheme research supports that wider tension between availability and actual need. [E6](sources.md#e6-scheme-operations-research)

Paula wants a clear personal spending limit, an honest explanation of compromises and confidence that the next car will be ready when the current one goes back. She is willing to consider another model or powertrain. She is less willing to discover the important catch after committing.

The proposed service brings a new question: if a reservation starts while she is driving to Farnborough or in a workshop, how much useful decision time does she really get? A two-hour hold reduces the refresh race, but its suitability for her working day still needs testing.

| **Thinks** | “What would I actually lose by changing?” “Will it take the bags?” “What does this cost me each month?” “How would charging work on an office day?” |

| **Does** | Plans office trips, checks the calendar before committing, compares alternatives with her Touring, returns to availability pages, and pauses an attractive choice to check cost, luggage and driver needs. |

## What this means for the experience

- **Start with what works.** Let Paula recognise her Touring and say what to preserve, change or correct. Observed use should prompt a question, not decide her lifestyle for her.
- **Keep setup light.** Start with two broad priorities and one optional improvement. Reuse known drivers, dates and practical needs; reveal missing checks when relevant. Ask only what can change the search, and keep essentials separate from preferences.
- **Remember her brief.** Keep looking while she gets on with her day. Updates should explain useful progress. A rejection stays visible with her reason and old comparison; it need not start the search again.
- **Make an offer assessable.** Compare practical fit with her current car, show points and cash clearly, surface unknowns and explain the remaining decision time.
- **Protect continuity and choice.** Make waiting and renewal risk visible. A reservation gives her room to decide; it does not authorise an order. An unsuitable alternative remains unsuitable when stock is scarce.

These are design implications for review against the [demo brief](../demo-brief.md), not additional agreed backlog scope. Validate Paula's working pattern, essential carrying needs, other-driver requirements, charging options and spending limit before treating the scenario as representative. Test success by whether she understands the trade-offs, retains essentials and avoids repeated checking or an avoidable gap in transport.
