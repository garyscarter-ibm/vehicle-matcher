## What story points can establish

The scaffolding policy defines points as relative complexity including test effort, explicitly not hours or model spend. The claiming session estimates each story before building and retains the estimate. This is useful scope evidence, but points are not automatically comparable with another team's estimates or velocity.

A traditional-team comparison needs a comparable baseline: the same acceptance criteria, similar starting assets and access, a shared estimation scale, and a stated quality standard. An independent human estimate made before implementation would help. Prior delivery data is stronger where available. Label an estimate as an estimate and an observed measurement as observed; do not claim a speed multiplier from incompatible points.

## Evidence to retain as work proceeds

| Dimension | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Accepted scope | Story ID, estimate before building, acceptance criteria and acceptance outcome. |
| Elapsed delivery | Start, review and acceptance times; show waiting and setup separately from active build. |
| Human effort | Russell/Gary time in discovery, briefing, implementation, review, correction and integration. Parallel agent wall time is not human effort saved by itself. |
| Quality and rework | Relevant checks, meaningful failures and fixes, review findings, acceptance rework and regressions. Test counts alone do not establish production readiness. |
| Reuse and adaptability | Identify inherited components/logic, what changed and what is new. Trace one genuine preference, rule or experience change through to its effect. |
| Model/tool cost | Record only available, attributable measurements. No verified Advantage Credit balance or per-story conversion is available. |

Capture discovery and orchestration effort as well as code production. Existing engine and component investment is part of the starting advantage and should be visible in the claim.
