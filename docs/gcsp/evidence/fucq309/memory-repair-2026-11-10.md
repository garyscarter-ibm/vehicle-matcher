## First-buddy boundary and normalization overlap

The actual canonical observer at 1,000 employees shows that the closed current store and pool become weakly dead during buddy bootstrap; the current run is also weakly dead before the first buddy allocation. That excludes retained closed-current raw storage at the observed failure stage. First buddy preparation starts with 50,841 singleton pool buckets, whose minimum container model is 2.03 MB, versus a 7.37 MB backing-store model. These are models of different costs, not additive heap measurements.

A direct canonical-policy first-buddy diagnostic at the original 128/16 MiB limits completes preparation. Its snapshot contains 84,325 objects and 12,087 arrays (10.04 MB container model), while preparation creates 29,016 contenders and 752 evaluation slices. Independent bounded contender chunks contain 174,096 object/array containers with a 16.94 MB model; slice chunks total a 10.04 MB model. Cross-chunk shared values may be counted again, and these counts exclude V8/string/index overhead. The earlier diagnostic's typed allocation failure is kept distinct from canonical OOM: the detailed probe logs its exact deliberately injected stop after preparation. An initial detailed probe failed at a non-unique source-hook anchor and is also retained.

Samples show 127.0 MiB used heap after plan digest, 104.6 MiB after its stored-row copy, and 119.8 MiB after the returned-row copy and before normalization. This locates the overlap between original draft data and newly normalized parents; it does not implicate bucket arrays as the principal cost.
