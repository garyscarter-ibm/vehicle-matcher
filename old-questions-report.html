The tag system — what labels exist on BMW cars and how they are assigned

Every car in the pool carries a set of character tags. These are not from the stock feed — they are computed by the engine from the car's model line, body type, fuel, and trim name. When you answer preference questions (style, priorities, primary use), the engine looks for cars whose tags match what you want. Here is every tag, what it means, and exactly which cars get it:

1 Budget
"What is your budget?"
Slider: £0–£150k		 Critical
62% / 250
car.priceMin
car.priceMax
 Hard ceiling: any car where priceMin > budget × 1.15 is eliminated entirely before scoring — it never even gets ranked. Cars between your min and max score 1.0 on budget. Cars below your min score lower (you set a floor for a reason). Cars slightly over budget survive but are flagged as a stretch. Runs first as a hard filter. At £40k, an X7 at £70k is gone. At £100k it is back. This is why budget changes the winner most — it literally changes which cars exist in the ranking. ESSENTIAL
Without this, a £20k buyer sees the same cars as a £150k buyer.

 Each priority adds a tag to the wanted set used by scoreCharacter, then boosts the weight of the matching engine dimension:

economy → wants 'efficient' tag, boosts economy weight ×1.5
performance → wants 'drivers-car' tag, boosts performance weight ×1.8
comfort → wants 'cruiser' tag, boosts character weight
tech → wants 'tech' tag, boosts character weight
image → wants 'image' tag, boosts character weight

A car scores 0.5 per matched tag (max 2 tags = score 1.0). 		Lives entirely in the taste half of the engine (20% of the blend). It personalises results among cars that are equally practical. Without it, two buyers with the same budget/fuel/body always get the same recommendation. KEEP
The personalisation layer. Removing it collapses the engine to a constraints-only ranker.

9 Annual mileage
"How many miles a year?"
Slider 1k–25k		 Low overall

24% at 20k+ miles
car.mpg (miles per gallon)
car.fuel
car.evRange
 Mileage creates a 0–1 scale that multiplies the economy weight:

Under 6k miles: economy weight barely increases — a thirsty petrol car is fine, running costs don't matter much.
8–13k miles: economy weight starts to climb.
20k+ miles: economy weight gets a full +1.0 boost. A 50mpg diesel or an EV now significantly outranks a 25mpg petrol of the same size.

Specifically: score = mpg-based score + (mpg-based score − 0.5) × mileage fraction. At high mileage, a frugal car rises, a thirsty one sinks. 		At under 8k miles/year this question barely matters — those buyers' fuel and body choices dominate. It's specifically designed for the high-mileage commuter who needs the economics to stack up. KEEP (simplify)
Only 11% overall but concentrated where it matters. Could simplify from a full slider to 3 bands (low/medium/high) without losing signal.

 Was: you answered small/medium/big, which set a target boot litres. The engine then scored how well the car's actual boot matched that target.

small → target ~200L (any car fine)
medium → target ~450L (5-door hatch, estate, smaller SUV)
big → target ~600L+ (estate, large SUV)

The problem: boot need is already implied by people + primaryUse. Solo city driver → small. Family on school runs → medium. Crew with roadtrips → big. The engine derives this automatically — the explicit question was asking for something it already knew. 		Simulation result: removing the boot question changed the winner in 0 out of 250 scenarios. Even in the 70% of cases where the stated boot answer disagreed with what people+primaryUse implied, the explicit boot answer still never moved the top car. The information was fully redundant. CUT — correctly
Zero information loss. Every signal it carried is already captured by people + primaryUse. Removing it saved a full screen without changing a single recommendation.

In 70% of scenarios the explicit boot answer differed from what people+primaryUse implied. But in every single one of those cases, the top recommendation was identical either way. The boot scorer was being overridden by stronger signals (body style, budget, fuel) every time. Safe to cut.

Mileage: only matters above 13k/year

Under 8,000 miles/year: mileage changes nothing — fuel and body choice dominate. Over 20,000: it shifts recommendations in 24% of cases, specifically moving diesel and EV above petrol. The full slider could be 3 bands (low/medium/high) without losing this signal.

Charging: 33% impact for EV buyers, 0% for petrol

The conditional logic (only show charging if you picked EV/PHEV/open) is exactly right. For petrol/diesel buyers it is invisible and irrelevant. For EV buyers without home charging, the engine rightly depresses EV scores — an EV relying on public chargers only is a very different car.
