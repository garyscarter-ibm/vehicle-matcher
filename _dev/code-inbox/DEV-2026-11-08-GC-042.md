  Found by FUCQ-217 (dev/DEV-2026-11-08-GC-034, head 2ea5f8df), 8 Nov 2026.

  **What happens:** when a brief has no charging answer, allocation uses the employee's stored charging fact, but the hold service treats charging as unknown. So the hold refuses every plug-in car allocation proposes, with `candidate-not-eligible`. That was 399 of 511 claim attempts at 150 employees. The wasted attempts are why a 1,000-employee comparison can't finish inside 30 minutes (3 of 9 releases, at 141, 350 and 653 s).

  **Decision built (the dispatcher's recommendation, Gary away; logged in docs/gcsp/overnight-log-2026-11-08.md):** make allocation judge charging the same way the hold service does. It's a small change in `server/gcsp/allocation/index.js`, and outcomes are the same because those holds were refused anyway.

  * Visible effect: for an employee with no charging answer, Buddy lists plug-in cars as needing confirmation rather than suitable.
  * The activity "suitable cars" count can fall, and follow-up can say no match instead of waiting. That's accurate, because the server would never hold those cars.
  * With charging aligned (scratch measurement), 1,000 employees finished in 15.1 min at 3.9 GB peak RSS.

  **Alternative (not built):** the hold service accepts the stored charging fact. That changes offers and comparison results.

  **Acceptance:**

  * Allocation and the hold service give the same eligibility verdict for charging in every brief state.
  * A test covers a brief with no charging answer and a plug-in car.
  * Comparison fixtures are re-pinned if they shift.
  * The 1,000-employee seed finishes.
